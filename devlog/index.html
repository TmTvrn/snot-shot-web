The Unreal port and the last hour

One day

The first commit in this repository landed at 1:17 PM on jam day. The entry was uploaded minutes before the 6:59 PM deadline. That is about five hours forty minutes for the web prototype, this dev log, the Unreal 5.9 port, the art references, the audio and the packaging.

What the Unreal build is

The whole game is one C++ actor running the same simulation as the prototype. Everything that moves is drawn with five instanced-mesh batches; the arena is a procedural mesh with a subsurface material. Sounds and music came from ElevenLabs, with several takes per sound that the game cycles through.

Model references

Each character got a front and back three-quarter reference image on a grey background, written as a full prompt and generated with ChatGPT. Those images went to Tripo to become 3D models. The floor and wall textures were made the same way and are in the build.

What went wrong at the end

The models did not ship

All six Tripo models were generated, at about 1.9 million triangles and 60 MB each. Only the hero and boss were cut down and imported in time. In the editor test the hero rendered as a black ball; in the packaged build the hero and boss were invisible. With fifteen minutes left the models were switched off and the game shipped with its original code-drawn characters.

8 GB of memory

The build machine has 8 GB of RAM. Shrinking two models in Blender while compiling Unreal pushed it deep into swap, and a job that should take two minutes ran for twelve without finishing. After that every heavy step ran one at a time.

Three packages in ten minutes

The first late package was stopped to add the models. The second had the invisible hero. The third, with models off, finished at 6:41 PM and is the one submitted.
